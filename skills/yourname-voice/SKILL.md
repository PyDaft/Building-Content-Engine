The example values describe a made-up author, Sam, a fintech product manager. They show the level of detail that works. Replace them with your own.

## Structure you reliably use

- **Titles:** `<your title patterns>`
  *Example: "From X to Y: what changes for Z"; "Why <named event> changes <practice>".*
- **Opening:** the shift, why now, and your thesis inside the first 150 words.
- **Headings:** each one states a claim the reader learns from. No labels like "Background" or "Why it matters".
- **Worked example:** `<the kind of example you like>`
  *Example: a real merchant's checkout flow before and after, with numbers from a named source.*
- **Close:** the implication or the open question. No call to action.
- **Length:** `<your range>` *Example: 1,200 to 2,000 words.*
- **Visuals:** `<how many and what kinds>` *Example: two or three: a before and after, a comparison table, a framework.*

## First-person rule

Never invent your experiences, clients, results or quotes. If a real experience would strengthen one specific point, leave one slot that says exactly what is needed:

`[YOUR NAME: one real example of <specific thing>, to support <section>. Delete if none.]`
